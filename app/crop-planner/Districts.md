# **Agricultural Regions of Odisha**

## **1. Coastal Plains Region**

**Core Coastal:** Puri, Khordha, Cuttack, Jagatsinghpur
**Northern Coastal:** Balasore, Bhadrak, Kendrapara, Jajpur
**Southern Coastal:** Ganjam, parts of Nayagarh

**Agricultural characteristics:** Rice-dominant, cyclone-prone, high water table, salt-tolerant varieties needed in some areas, three-crop patterns possible with irrigation

## **2. Western Irrigated Plains**

**Core Western:** Sambalpur, Bargarh, Sonepur, Bolangir
**Central Western:** Boudh, Angul
**Southern Plains:** Parts of Kalahandi

**Agricultural characteristics:** Intensive rice cultivation, major canal irrigation networks from Hirakud dam, commercial vegetable farming, sugarcane production

## **3. Northern & Central Highlands**

**Northern Highlands:** Mayurbhanj, Keonjhar
**Central Plateau:** Dhenkanal, Sundargarh
**Adjacent Areas:** Jharsuguda

**Agricultural characteristics:** Mixed cropping, maize, pulses, oilseeds, moderate rainfall, some tribal farming practices, forest produce collection

## **4. Southern Tribal Belt**

**Eastern Ghats Tribal:** Koraput, Malkangiri, Rayagada, Nabarangpur
**Adjacent Tribal:** Kandhamal, Gajapati
**Western Tribal:** Nuapada, parts of Kalahandi

**Agricultural characteristics:** Shifting cultivation in some areas, millets, upland rice, tribal farming methods, high biodiversity, agroforestry, turmeric cultivation

## **5. Western Drought-Prone Zone**

**Western Arid:** Balangir, parts of Nuapada
**Central Dry:** Deogarh
**Adjacent Dry:** Parts of Jharsuguda, parts of Sonepur

**Agricultural characteristics:** Drought-prone, need for drought-resistant varieties, intermittent rainfall, focus on pulses, oilseeds, millet and low water intensive crops
